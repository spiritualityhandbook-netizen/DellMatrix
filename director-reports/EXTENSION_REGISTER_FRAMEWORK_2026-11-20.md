## 1. The problem this framework exists to prevent

The program already carries the disease this framework treats, three
times over:

1. **The GP-G collision.** The Unified §4 register's GP-G01..GP-G28
   and the MWL's own "G-" source IDs are different registers sharing a
   prefix (MWL row #15's source ID is "G-04"; GP-G04 is Water &
   Growth). Two registers, one namespace, silent confusion.
2. **The invisible 22.** Twenty-two §4 items have no MWL row. Work
   that exists only in one register falls out of every count built on
   the other — the board stood at 39/182 for days while credited work
   sat unrecorded, the exact failure the 2026-11-20 settlement (PR
   #500) repaired for R17/Q-LC.1.
3. **The denominator drift.** Unified totals have been stated as 224,
   227, and 182 at different times by different documents. Each was
   honestly derived; none carried its derivation on its face, so the
   numbers read as contradictions instead of different instruments.

New objectives will keep arriving (F-class extensions, FI intakes,
census findings). Without an admission framework, each arrival repeats
all three failures.

## 2. The rules

**R1 — Distinct IDs, checked against BOTH registers.** A new extension
objective receives its ID only after a collision check against (a)
the MWL (`master_work_list_items.json`, by `n`, `ids`, and title
keywords) and (b) the GP-G ledger (Part III's disposition ledger).
The check is recorded in the item's intake entry: what was searched,
what near-matches exist, why the new ID does not collide.

**R2 — No renumbering, ever.** The original 72 inventory rows keep
their numbers permanently. The MWL's `n` sequence is append-only.
An extension item maps to existing rows by *reference* (a `maps_to`
list), never by insertion, splitting, or renumbering. A retired row's
number is never reused.

**R3 — Namespace separation.** Each register owns a prefix, stated on
every use: `MWL #n` (work list), `GP-Gnn` (Unified §4), `GP-Enn`
(Edition 1 circuits), `FAR-n` (§7 stratum), `FI-nnn` (future-item
intakes), `UU-n` (unified unknowns). A bare "G-12" in any new document
is a defect — the GP-G23 naming discipline applied to registers
themselves.

**R4 — Mapping is topical, never by ID.** A cross-register mapping
asserts "same defect/work" and cites the topical basis in one line.
Mappings never import the other register's *status*: GP-G03 maps to
MWL #15 topically, but its ledger entry had to resolve the two
registers' conflicting status claims on evidence, not by copying
#15's COMPLETE. (This rule is the general form of the Unified Part V
§3a method.)

**R5 — No duplicate closure credit.** An item that maps to an MWL row
earns its COMPLETE exactly once, in the row. A register-only item
(GP-G class) earns its closure in its own ledger, and that closure is
a *register* closure (COMPLETE / OWNED-AND-CLOSED / RETIRED WITH
RECEIPT under the §4 closing condition) — it does not mint an MWL
point. Conversely, closing an MWL row does not silently close a
mapped register item whose scope is broader than the row (cf. G-17
vs MWL #72: the row isolates a ledger; the item is a classification —
distinct units, separately closed).

**R6 — Every total carries its derivation.** Any stated program total
names: the register(s) counted, the snapshot SHA, the date, and the
inclusion rule (e.g. "182 = MWL active rows at af2519b6, SANDBOXED
excluded"). A total without its derivation is a claim, not a count.

**R7 — Intake is a filing, not a build.** An extension objective enters
as a register entry with: ID (per R1), one-line objective, source
(who filed it, when, under what authority), home Part, and initial
status. Entry confers no build authorization, no credit, and no
priority — placement and authorization remain the authorities' acts
(the FI-017 pattern: intake logged, placement decided, build gated).

**R8 — The register write rides inside the closing package.** When an
extension item closes, its register update lands in the same PR that
closes it — never as a later settlement. (Standing commitment since
PR #500; stated here so extension items inherit it by rule, not by
reminder.)

The 28 GP-G items are this framework's first exhibits; the disposition

- **22 unmapped items:** R1's negative result recorded per item ("no
  MWL row carries this work — checked by title, source IDs, and
  topical search"); they live in the GP-G ledger under R5's
  register-closure rule.
- **GP-G29/G30/G31:** R3 in action — they are §7 provisional-register
  items (SD-GP-01 quarantine), NOT §4 items; the framework keeps them
  in Part IV's namespace and out of the §4 closing condition, with the
  FAR-17 ≡ GP-G30 dual-filing resolved by reference, not by deleting
  either seat.
- **FI-017:** an R7 intake that predates the framework — logged
  2026-11-16, placement decided by the Director, development carried
  in Part IV. Under the framework it would additionally carry its
  collision check on its face; the ledger notes it as a conforming
  intake with that one field retro-recorded by reference to its
  register row of record (goal-workspace decision register, line 127).

## 4. What changes when this framework is confirmed

1. Every future intake (F-class, FI-class, census-born) is filed
   under R1–R8 or it is not filed at all.
2. The GP-G ledger and the MWL reconcile by *rule* at each Part
   landing (the Part III ledger's snapshot rule), instead of by
   periodic emergency settlement.
3. Scoreboard statements follow R6 — the next "how many are done?"
   question gets one number with its derivation attached, or the
   honest statement that the registers disagree and where.

## 5. Non-goals

- This framework renumbers nothing, closes nothing by itself, and
  authorizes no build.
- It does not merge the two registers into one file: the MWL stays
  the scoreboard's source; the GP-G ledger stays the §4 closing
  condition's instrument. One authority per register, mappings between
  them — the no-second-authority law applied to bookkeeping.
