## PART I — THE NEURON, AS MAN KNOWS IT (evidence-graded)

**N1. A neuron is a threshold with a memory of weights.**
It sums weighted inputs arriving on thousands of synapses
and fires when the sum crosses a threshold; the weights are
the learning. (Established.) *Dell note: this is 73[Threshold]
wearing meat — sum, compare, fire — with 74[Weight] on every
incoming edge.*

**N2. Plasticity is local and timing-ruled (Hebb / STDP).**
Synapses strengthen when firing is correlated, and spike-
timing-dependent plasticity sharpens it: pre-before-post by
~tens of milliseconds strengthens; the reverse order weakens.
The synapse never sees the whole brain — it learns from
LOCAL events and their TIMING. (Established.) *Credit is
assigned by clocks, not by committees.*

**N3. One neuron is not one switch — dendrites compute.**
Dendritic branches perform their own nonlinear sub-
computations before the soma votes; a single human cortical
neuron can implement functions (e.g. XOR-class) that textbook
models need a small network for (Gidon et al., 2020, and the
dendritic-computation literature). (Established, still
deepening.) *The unit is a committee, not a bit.*

**N4. Neuromodulators are slow broadcasts that retune
learning itself.** Dopamine (reward-prediction error),
acetylcholine (attention/encoding), noradrenaline (arousal),
serotonin — diffuse, volume-broadcast signals that change
the RULES (learning rate, plasticity windows) everywhere at
once, on slow timescales. (Established.) *This is the body's
TUNE organ, chemical edition — and it tunes the LEARNING,
not the content.*

**N5. Two memory systems, deliberately mismatched speeds.**
Complementary learning systems: the hippocampus learns FAST
(one-shot episodes, exact) while the neocortex learns SLOW
(statistical, general) — and during sleep, hippocampal
sharp-wave ripples REPLAY the day's episodes, compressed
~10–20×, interleaved, teaching the cortex. Forgetting and
generalization are features of the transfer, not failures.
(Established framework; replay function still researched.)
*Fast exact store + slow general store + offline replay
transfer: the brain's two-store heart, with a night shift.*

**N6. Oscillations bind; synchrony is a routing signal.**
Brain rhythms (theta ~4–8 Hz, gamma ~30–80 Hz) nest inside
each other; regions that synchronize exchange information
preferentially ("communication through coherence"). Phase
acts like an address window: fire in the right phase or
your signal lands in a closed gate. (Established framework.)
*Rhythm is not decoration on the signal; rhythm IS routing.*

**N7. The brain runs near criticality — the edge of chaos.**
Neuronal avalanches distribute as power laws; the cortex
appears poised near a phase transition between order and
disorder, maximizing dynamic range and information capacity.
(Strong evidence, debated edges.) *Not too stiff, not too
loose: capability lives at the boundary.*

**N8. Prediction is the cortex's native tongue.** Predictive-
processing accounts: higher levels constantly predict lower
levels' input; only the ERROR travels up. Perception and
learning are error-accounting systems. (Major framework,
contested scope.) *What flows upward is surprise — the rest
was already known.*

**C2. Timing-ruled credit (STDP for flows).** A flow that

**C4. Neuromodulatory TUNE.** TUNE stops retuning content

**C5. Phase routing on the bus.** The heartbeat gains

**C6. The double-pendulum duo, made honest.** DuoBeta's two

**C7. Error-only upward flow (the prediction organ).**

**Grade B.**

**C8. Dendritic organs.** An organ is not a relay: it

**C9. Criticality watch.** Profile gains a regime gauge:

boundary, N7's lesson. **Grade V** (measure plausible;
intervention law unwritten).

**C10. Cross-host synchronization (Huygens' beam).** Two
