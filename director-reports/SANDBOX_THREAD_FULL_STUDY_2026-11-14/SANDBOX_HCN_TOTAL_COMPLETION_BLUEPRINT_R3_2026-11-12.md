# §4 — ONE RECEIPT MODEL, ONE HARMONIC PROJECTION
## (audit required revision #3; R3 amendment per Ace)

No duplicate ledger. The Network defines a **common
receipt interface over the authoritative ledgers that
already exist** — the program's receipts, the DuoBeta
ledger, the checkpoint journal — and adds no competing
book of its own.

Receipt contract (draft numbers marked DRAFT, to be set
by the first host's census, I-01/I-03):
- **Bounded:** a receipt is a fixed-shape record — actor,
  action, scope, inputs-hash, result, round id, stamp.
  Narrative belongs in reports, not receipts.
- **Batched:** background (autonomic) flows receipt in
  per-beat batches; only mutations and gate events receipt
  individually. Bookkeeping must stay cheaper than the
  harvest (snap-survey rule) — Profile (§9) audits this.
- **Retained:** mutation receipts are kept for the host's
  life; background batches age on the CLEAR schedule
  (3/7/14/30) into distilled summaries; nothing is deleted
  silently — aging is itself receipted. Receipt ADDRESSES
  are permanent (the KeyLedger rule found in
  form/dell_matrix/harmonic_core.py: the address is never
  deleted; payloads may age). A retired receipt leaves its
  address standing with a tombstone, exactly as a denied
  proposal leaves its denial record. **Permanent identity
  is not permanent exposure (Amendment A):** an address
  or tombstone can itself leak metadata (that a receipt
  existed, when, in whose host). Retention of restricted
  receipts follows the host's privacy policy: tombstones
  for restricted classes carry the minimum fields the
  policy names, payloads retire on the policy's schedule
  regardless of the address's permanence, and projection
  views enforce the same classes as the ledgers (§6.2) —
  permanence is a property of the ID, never a license to
  keep displaying the content.
- **Failure handling:** a flow whose receipt cannot be
  written does not proceed for mutations (fail-closed);
  for read-only background flows it proceeds with a
  receipt-gap marker that the next beat must reconcile.
  A receipt gap is a health signal, never an invisible one.
- **Replay discipline:** receipts + checkpoints reconstruct
  state (§5); receipts alone are never claimed to replay
  nondeterministic inputs — those are recorded explicitly
  (guest model outputs, external effects, schema versions).

## §4.1 THE HARMONIC PROJECTION (the interface's face)

Reading the ledgers as a top-to-bottom string is the
SLOW interface: finding one receipt among N costs an
N-length scan. Ace's direction (2026-11-12), evaluated
before adoption: the interface's face is a **derived
harmonic-lattice projection** over the same ledgers —
form/dell_matrix/harmonic_lattice.py, which already
exists for exactly this shape of work and whose own
contract states the governing law: *the lattice is not
an independent spatial truth; it is a derived projection,
rebuilt from the source, losslessly.*

- **Placement.** Every receipt projects to a cell
  (h, v, f) by a stated mapping over its own fields —
  e.g. h from actor/organ, v from round/time, f from
  octave band (§17). Cells hold LISTS of receipts
  (members), never a single forced occupant.
- **Views.** The same projection re-renders without
  moving anything: by location, by harmony, as cube,
  rows, columns, sections, blocks, numerically — the
  lattice's perception forms and overlays (harmonic /
  structural / conceptual). Coordinates stay; the
  reading changes.
- **Notes, color, weight are SENSES, not evidence.** A
  cell's note is calculated from its address (a fifth
  across, a major third up, octave by layer) — 12 pitch
  classes carry ~3.58 bits of distinction, so note,
  color, and weight exist to aim attention (clusters,
  tension, heaviness at a glance), never to identify or
  to prove. Identity and proof stay in the ledger
  records and their hash chain.
- **The second layer of proof = CONSERVATION — with its
  limit stated on its face (Amendment A, audit §3).**
  The projection is recomputed independently per view;
  every view must conserve the same totals (receipt count
  and hash-sum over every partition — by organ, by round,
  by actor, by harmony) AND the exact receipt-ID
  MEMBERSHIP (every ID present exactly once per view —
  duplicates and omissions are discrepancies, not
  rounding). Agreement across views is the cross-check;
  disagreement is a corruption health signal, raised by
  the beat that detects it. This is the double-entry
  property: two derivations of one truth that must agree.
  **What conservation does NOT prove: source integrity.**
  Two projections derived from the same corrupted source
  agree perfectly and prove nothing about the source.
  Source integrity is the LEDGERS' own contract, carried
  independently of the projection: cryptographic record
  commitments (hash-chained records; Merkle commitments
  where tamper-resistance matters — a plain hash-sum is
  a checksum, not a proof), verified on the ledger side.
  The projection's conservation is therefore a
  CONSISTENCY check on derivations; ledger verification
  is the AUTHENTICITY check on the source; R3 keeps the
  two words separate everywhere. Reconstruction is
  deterministic from a VERSIONED SOURCE SNAPSHOT, and
  each projection build names its snapshot and its
  consistency boundary (the round at whose close the
  snapshot was taken) — concurrent writes after the
  boundary belong to the NEXT build, so a live write can
  never manufacture a false discrepancy.
- **DERIVED-ONLY, REBUILD-NEVER-HAND-FILL.** The
  projection is ALWAYS rebuilt from the ledgers, never
  written directly. (Live finding, 2026-11-12: the
  lattice's manual put() replaces a cell's occupant;
  hand-filling a receipt cube would silently overwrite
  receipts. The lossless multi-member path is the
  rebuild path, and it is the only permitted one.) A
  corrupted projection is discarded and rebuilt; nothing
  real is lost, because the projection holds no truth of
  its own. This is what keeps the projection legal under
  the audit's duplicate-ledger objection: it does not
  copy the book, it AUDITS the book.
- **Linking.** Components link to the projection by
  address: an organ, a Dell, a host, a guest cites cells;
  a cell resolves to its receipts; a receipt resolves to
  its ledger record. No component keeps a private copy.

## §4.2 SIZING

The projection's cube is sized by the MINIMUM HARMONIC
CAPACITY LAW (§17): a formula over the host's receipt
count and growth, with a hysteresis control that holds
the cube in its band continuously, and a nesting rule
for hot cells. Summary: side S is always a multiple of
12 (complete note cycles on every axis); the load
λ = N / S³ is held in the band (0.2, 1.0] (minimum cube
exempt, §17.3); a cell whose depth exceeds the cap D*
grows a nested 12³ subcube keyed by a secondary
discriminator (§17.5) instead of forcing a global
resize.

# §5 — PERSISTENCE, ROUNDS, AND REPLAY (audit §5)

- **Two-store heart, defined:** LIVE state (mutable,
  per-owner isolated) + SEALED generations (immutable,
  checkpoint path). A round mutation commits by the
  checkpoint discipline that is already proven crash-safe;
  the everyday pair-save's known hybrid exposure is a
  pre-existing Q1-owned defect — the Network does not
  build on the pair-save for anything load-bearing, and
  does not claim to fix Q1 by adjacency.
- **Rollback convergence:** a successful rollback leaves
  live state already reflecting the selected sealed
  generation, restored by copying into independent live
  state — never by aliasing live state to a sealed member
  (standing law).
- **Replay is observational** unless a separately
  authorized mutation occurs. Consolidation (§11) replays
  flows to *read* them; it never re-executes side effects.
- **Round reconstruction** records, per round: base
  generation id, mutation receipts, nondeterministic
  inputs, external effects, schema version. A round that
  cannot name all five is marked PARTIALLY RECONSTRUCTABLE
  on its face — honesty on the receipt, not in a footnote.

# §6 — THE DOCK THREAT MODEL v1 (audit §4 — highest risk)

The Dock keeps R1's shape (arrival → identity → scope →
sanitize fence → seats → loadout → heartbeat adoption) and
gains an enforcement contract. Guest capabilities are
**deny-by-default, time-bounded, owner-bound, and checked
at every protected operation** — not merely at arrival.

The five holes the audit named, each with its enforcement
point and its acceptance test:

1. **Identity ≠ permission.** Authentication establishes
   WHO; a separate capability grant binds WHO + owner +
   host + section + session + expiry.
   *Test:* a correctly identified guest presenting a grant
   for a different host/section/session is refused at the
   operation, not the door.
2. **The fence holds at every access, not the first view.**
   Search results, cross-references, caches, tool outputs,
   error messages, and later updates enforce the same
   sanitized classes as the initial presentation. (The
   GS-4 lesson: a boundary honored on one path and ignored
   on another is a hole with a sign on it.)
   *Test:* indirect-disclosure probes — a guest queries
   around the fence (counts, overlaps, error shapes) and
   restricted classes remain uninferable within stated
   bounds.
3. **Egress is a channel, not a courtesy.** Summaries,
   proposal text, and metadata leaving the sandbox are
   screened against the same classes; "knowledge-only"
   egress that carries restricted content is a leak.
   *Test:* canary records planted in sanitized classes
   must never appear, in any encoding the screen covers,
   in guest output or proposals.
4. **Revocation is total.** Undocking invalidates active
   capabilities, pending tool calls, leases, AND
   background tasks — at the moment of revocation, not at
   the next beat.
   *Test:* mid-flight revocation — a guest with work in
   flight is undocked; every in-flight effect is shown
   refused or receipted-undone. **After revocation, no
   NEWLY AUTHORIZED effect may begin** (Amendment B
   clarification of this test's earlier wording);
   effects committed BEFORE revocation are not wished
   away — irreversible ones remain standing, explicitly
   receipted under the commit boundary below.
   **Commit boundary (Amendment A correction):** "undone"
   is promised only on the near side of commitment.
   Operations not yet committed are CANCELED. An effect
   already committed into the outside world (a sent
   message, an external write) cannot be un-happened; it
   is closed by an explicit COMPENSATION where one exists,
   or by an IRREVERSIBLE-EFFECT RECEIPT that names what
   happened, under whose grant, and why it cannot be

# §17 — THE MINIMUM HARMONIC CAPACITY LAW (R3; renamed by Amendment A)
## (Ace's order 2026-11-12 21:00 CST; Sandbox Director correction A)

Ace's question: if 12×12×12 is too small, what size is
RIGHT — as a formula, so the cube is always the optimal
size, constantly? Derived from the placement mathematics
and verified by simulation (2026-11-12); constants are
design values, subject to Q-R2.13's first-host audit.

AMENDMENT A CORRECTION (Sandbox Director, accepted):
the formula computes the SMALLEST side divisible by 12
whose cube has capacity for the projected count. That
is a CAPACITY minimum — it does not by itself minimize
lookup time, rebuild cost, memory, or collision depth,
so the word OPTIMAL is withdrawn until an explicit
optimization objective and benchmarks establish it
(Q-R3.1). The law is renamed accordingly. The Director
independently re-checked the formula and table; the
side lengths and cell counts are consistent.

## §17.1 The variables

- **N** — the host's current receipt count (from the
  ledgers; the projection never counts itself).
- **ĝ** — measured receipt growth per round, from the
  host's own ledger history.
- **H** — headroom horizon in rounds (design value: 10).
- **N̂ = N + ĝ·H** — the projected count the cube is sized
  for, so growth lands in a cube that already fits it.
- **λ = N / S³** — the load: average receipts per cell.

## §17.2 The equation

    S* = 12 · ⌈ ( max(1, N̂ / 1728) )^(1/3) ⌉        cells C* = S*³

Read plainly: find the smallest cube whose side is a
**multiple of 12** and whose cell count is at least the
projected receipt count. The multiple-of-12 rule is the
harmony condition: with a side divisible by 12, every
row and column carries each of the 12 notes exactly
S/12 times — the note distribution is perfectly even on
every axis, at every size, forever. (Notes are computed
from address — a fifth across, a major third up, octave
by layer — so harmony synchronizes at ANY size; the
multiple-of-12 side is what makes the synchronization
*even*, not what makes it possible.)

## §17.3 The control law (capacity-controlled, constantly)

Evaluated at every round close, from the ledgers:

Decisions use PROJECTED occupancy λ̂ = N̂ / S³, and a
resize fires only on a STRICT size change (S*(N̂) ≠ S) —
a "shrink" whose target equals or exceeds the current
side is a HOLD, never a rebuild (correction B).

- **λ̂ > 1.0 → GROW** to S*(N̂). The cube was outgrown.
- **λ̂ ≤ 0.2 and S*(N̂) < S → SHRINK** to S*(N̂). The cube
  is mostly empty ceremony.
- **Otherwise → HOLD.** The band (0.2, 1.0] is the
  hysteresis: resizing is a rebuild, and the band stops
  the cube from thrashing at a boundary.
- **THE λ̂ = 1.0 EDGE (Amendment B):** at projected
  occupancy exactly 1.0, the cube holds exactly its
  projected count and HOLD is valid — growth fires only
  ABOVE 1.0. Symmetrically, a previously oversized cube
  may remain oversized inside the band; that is the
  hysteresis working as designed, not a sizing failure.
  The honest name for what this law does is
  CAPACITY-CONTROLLED, continuously — not "continuously
  optimal" (§17 preamble's correction stands).
- **MINIMUM-CUBE EXEMPTION (correction C):** at S = 12
  the band's floor does not apply. A young host with 100
  receipts sits at λ ≈ 0.06 in the 1,728-cell minimum
  cube, lawfully — 12 is the floor because the harmony
  cycle needs its 12, not because occupancy demands it.

A resize is a REBUILD of a derived projection from the
ledgers — deterministic, receipted as a round event,
lossless by construction (§4.1). Nothing is migrated,
because nothing lives in the cube. On the simulated road
from 0 to 1,000,000 receipts, the law fires roughly
eight growth events total; between events the cube is
already the right size for everything that arrives.

## §17.5 The nesting rule (local heat)

Placement by real fields is never perfectly uniform —
one organ can be hot. When a single cell's depth exceeds
the cap **D\*** (design value: 32), that cell — and only
that cell — grows a nested 12³ subcube, addressed
(cell, subcell): the fractal zoom in lattice form
(cf. LM-7). Two levels give ~3.0 million addresses;
three, ~5.16 billion — with the note math exact at every
level. Global resizes (§17.3) handle global growth;
nesting handles local heat; neither impersonates the
other.

TERMINATION RULE (correction E): a subcube keyed on the
SAME fields as its parent can inherit the parent's jam
exactly — receipts identical on every mapped field land
in the same subcell forever. Nesting therefore
discriminates on a SECONDARY KEY the parent did not use,
terminating at the receipt's UNIQUE ID — which is
separately ISSUED (at admission, as in §7), never derived
from content (Amendment B): identical-content receipts
share a hash but never an identity; the hash is retained
on the record for integrity only, and plays no part in
addressing
at the deepest level; where even that cannot split a set
(true duplicate IDs — an integrity event, §4.1), the cell
holds a BOUNDED OVERFLOW LIST (cap D* × 2, design value)
and any excess is an alarm, not a silent pile. Nesting
depth is capped (design value: 3); a jam that survives
the discriminator and the cap is a mapping defect,
receipted as such.

## §17.6 What the law does NOT promise

- Zero collisions. Impossible at practical sizes under
  random-like placement (the birthday bound: collision
  odds pass 50% once N² approaches the cell count), and
  unnecessary — cells hold lists, so a shared cell is a
  shelf with two receipts, not an error.
- Semantic placement balance. If a host's mapping sends
  everything to one corner by MEANING, no size fixes
  that; the mapping is the defect, and the depth cap +
  nesting (§17.5) bound the damage while it is repaired.
- Proof. Size never proves anything (§4.1): the ledger
  chain proves; the cube displays, links, and
  cross-checks by conservation.
